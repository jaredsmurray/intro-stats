# Active adjudication and revision rules

These are durable patterns supported by completed chapter-review rounds. They
guide intervention choice; they do not replace mathematical verification or
author judgment.

## Diagnose before writing

- State what the chapter already teaches successfully before naming a residual
  failure. Never turn a missing local derivation into a claim that the broader
  concept is absent.
- Preserve student friction even when technical verification reframes its
  cause. A misleading bridge can be the real defect when two correct quantities
  merely differ.
- Technical correctness does not establish pedagogical clarity. A technically
  clean sentence can still create a predictable misconception.
- Treat replicated agent agreement as corroboration, not proof.
- Require a failed objective-bearing student task, direct contradiction, or
  reproduced correctness problem before promoting an omission into structured
  findings.

## Choose the repair at the right level

- Consider deleting a throwaway claim before qualifying or quantifying it.
- Repair tables, figures, captions, computations, or data provenance when they
  are the source of the contradiction.
- Prefer a structural fix to a disclaimer when units, scales, or concepts are
  misaligned.
- Restore suitable commented author prose before inventing a new explanation.
- Do not manufacture an example solely to give a formula an application.
- When several local findings share a cause, propose a plan-shaped resolution.

## Protect the book's scope and voice

- Chapters are code-free by design; computing mechanisms belong in coding
  supplements.
- Use plain, precise prose. Avoid playful metaphors, cute language, punchy
  antitheses, and slogan-like closers.
- Do not overexplain a correct statement merely to reconcile every advanced
  nuance. Record deliberate omissions so later reviews do not relitigate them.
- Recommend no change when the chapter already meets the objective and the
  proposed addition mainly serves completeness or reviewer reassurance.
- Quarantine an optional prerequisite in a short parenthetical rather than
  replacing a correct mathematical statement with a looser metaphor.

## Verify the repair

- Use reader-visible values to verify displayed arithmetic.
- Treat source precision scans and rendered arithmetic evaluation as
  complementary evidence. Report their actual scope; zero findings with thin
  or unreported coverage is not a clean numeric audit.
- Compute data-dependent counts and constants inline rather than hardcoding
  them.
- Inspect a rendered figure before asserting what shape it shows.
- Inspect the original-resolution image before promoting any clipping,
  legibility, or visual-content claim.
- Recheck neighboring claims after changing display precision; a local rounding
  repair can break the next sentence.
- Convert recurring, objectively checkable conventions into tooling.

## Escalate deliberately

- Hold claims whose truth depends on data provenance or external sources.
- Give cross-chapter plans an owner, affected chapters, and unblock condition.
- Keep terminology-ledger approval separate from chapter-fix approval.
- Distinguish `not substantiated`, `true but immaterial`, `out of scope`,
  `repair rejected`, and `escalated to plan`; they teach different lessons.
